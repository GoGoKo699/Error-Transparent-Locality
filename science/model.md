# Logical evolution through correctable errors

An error during a logical gate can change the Hamiltonian seen by the encoded information. Correcting the error at the end then need not repair the angle accumulated between the fault and recovery. Error-transparent evolution prevents this timing dependence: the same logical generator acts before and after every protected error.

This account asks how many physical qubits must participate in an individual interaction, and how much total Pauli coupling strength that requirement costs. It develops a general lower bound, an exact four-body construction, and a comparison with the established five-qubit protected Hamiltonian. The error-transparent and protected-Hamiltonian frameworks themselves are prior work; see the [related-work account](../references/RELATED_WORK.md).

## 1. Encoded space and detection distance

Let $V:\mathbb C^K\to(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes n}$ be an isometry with $K\ge2$, and let $P=VV^\dagger$ be its code projector. A tensor Pauli is an element of $\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes n}$, without an additional phase. Its weight is the number of nonidentity factors.

The detection distance $d$ means that every Pauli $Q$ of weight below $d$ has scalar compression:

```math
V^\dagger QV=q_Q I_K,\qquad \mathrm{wt}(Q)\lt d.
```

No stabilizer, purity, or nondegeneracy assumption enters the general bounds. For the full set of errors acting on at most $t$ qubits to be correctable, assume $d\ge2t+1$. Indeed, products $E_a^\dagger E_b$ of two errors of weight at most $t$ have weight at most $2t$, so their compressions obey the Knill–Laflamme condition. Linear combinations and channels generated by this error set are consequently correctable as well.

The explicit examples encode one qubit. The general statements allow an arbitrary finite logical dimension.

## 2. Exact error transparency

Take a Hermitian physical Hamiltonian $H$ preserving the code, and define its logical action $h_L=V^\dagger HV$, so $HV=Vh_L$. The strict transparency condition is

```math
\text{(M1)}\qquad HEV=EVh_L
\quad\text{for every tensor Pauli }E\text{ with }\mathrm{wt}(E)\le t.
```

This is an equality of maps into the full physical Hilbert space. Matching only the compressed matrices does not establish it: an omitted component could leak out of an error sector. The construction and finite verifiers check the full equality.

The lower bounds also allow consistent real scalar shifts,

```math
\text{(M2)}\qquad HEV=EVh_L+\lambda_E EV,\qquad \lambda_I=0.
```

Such shifts change the phase of an error sector but leave its logical energy differences unchanged. Degeneracy or overlap between error images can restrict the permitted shifts; their consistency is an assumption, not an independent control freedom. Equation (M1) holds with every shift zero for the constructions used here.

These conditions are pointwise conditions on the generator. A gate that is correctable only at selected final angles, a changing code, and a dissipative correction protocol that mixes syndrome sectors are different models.

## 3. A fault at an unknown time

Set $\hbar=1$. For a constant strictly transparent Hamiltonian and a fault $E$ at any time $0\le\tau\le T$, exponentiation of (M1) gives

```math
\text{(M3)}\qquad e^{-iH(T-\tau)}E e^{-iH\tau}V
=EVe^{-ih_LT}.
```

The accumulated logical unitary is independent of $\tau$. A final ideal recovery removes $E$. The same reasoning applies to a common pulse envelope $H(t)=g(t)H_\star$, replacing the duration by its integrated envelope. More generally, (M1) can be imposed at every instant for a fixed code and a time-dependent logical Hamiltonian; the corresponding time-ordered propagators satisfy the same intertwining relation.

Under (M2), the right-hand side of (M3) acquires a scalar phase $e^{-i\lambda_E(T-\tau)}$. For a coherent combination of protected errors, this can change relative syndrome amplitudes, but they remain in the same correctable error span. Ideal recovery still restores the logical evolution. The stronger strict equality is useful because it also preserves the physical error representative without that phase change.

For multiple instantaneous faults, the strict argument can be iterated as long as every accumulated error lies in the protected set of errors of weight at most $t$. The claims do not posit a finite-error-rate threshold, fault-free recovery hardware, or protection against arbitrary higher-weight correlated faults.

## 4. Interaction order and coupling budget

Expand $H$ uniquely in the standard Hermitian tensor-Pauli basis, whose nonidentity elements have eigenvalues $\pm1$:

```math
H=\sum_Q h_Q Q=\sum_{w=0}^r H_w,\qquad h_Q\in\mathbb R.
```

Two resources are kept separate:

| Quantity | Definition | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum interaction order | $r=\max_{h_Q\ne0}\mathrm{wt}(Q)$ | Largest number of qubits in one Pauli interaction |
| Native coupling budget | $J=\sum_{Q\ne I}\lvert h_Q\rvert$ | Sum of absolute strengths of native Pauli interactions |
| Logical half-splitting | $\omega=[\lambda_{\max}(h_L)-\lambda_{\min}(h_L)]/2$ | Nontrivial logical energy scale |

An identity term can be removed freely and is excluded from $J$. Including it would only increase the stated lower bounds. For a logical qubit with $h_L=\Omega Z$ plus a scalar, $\omega=|\Omega|$; the Bloch-sphere angular velocity is $2|\Omega|$.

Interaction order is different from the union of all supports and from geometric range. The four-body construction uses nine data qubits. Coupling budget is different from the operator norm of $H$: commuting terms can cancel in the logical action and have a substantial absolute coefficient sum. All budget claims refer to this fixed physical Pauli basis. A hardware implementation with different primitive controls needs its own accounting.

## 5. Statements supported by this model

The [locality theorem](locality.md) proves $r\ge d+t$ whenever $h_L$ is nontrivial. For a code correcting every error of weight at most $t$, this implies $r\ge3t+1$. This is a necessary bound; general attainability is not asserted.

At the smallest allowed interaction order, the [coupling theorem](coupling_cost.md) determines the logical contribution of every weight sector. In the single-error case it also gives a bound for arbitrary $r>d$:

```math
J\ge\frac{r+d}{r-d}\,\omega.
```

The [nine-qubit construction](four_body_construction.md) attains $r=4$ and $J=7\omega$ at $d=3,t=1$. The canonical [five-qubit benchmark](coupling_cost.md#the-five-qubit-benchmark) attains $r=5$ and $J=4\omega$. Their comparison therefore separates minimizing interaction order from minimizing the strength required at a given order.

The [control account](control_robustness.md) treats coefficient errors and pulse schedules directly. In particular, commutation of ideal terms does not justify applying them sequentially when faults may occur during the sequence.

Preparation, syndrome extraction, final recovery, and synthesis of three- or four-body interactions from other hardware primitives are outside the native Hamiltonian budget. The exact statements remain useful without identifying that budget with a complete implementation cost.
