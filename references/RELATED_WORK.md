# Relation to prior work

This project studies exact continuous logical evolution generated by a static Hamiltonian on a fixed quantum code. Its resources are the largest weight of a term in the standard physical tensor-Pauli expansion and the sum of the absolute nonidentity coefficients in that expansion. The comparisons below distinguish these resources and the required error guarantee from earlier error-transparent, autonomous, and energy-penalty constructions. Bibliographic entries are in [references.bib](references.bib).

## Model and results being compared

Let $V$ encode a logical space into physical qubits, and let the code detect every Pauli of weight less than $d$. For every tensor-Pauli error $E$ of weight at most $t$, require

```math
HV=Vh_L,\qquad HEV=EVh_L+\lambda_E EV.
```

The logical generator $h_L$ is nonscalar. Consistent real offsets $\lambda_E$ change branch phases but preserve logical energy differences. The lower bounds allow these offsets; the supplied constructions satisfy the strict form $\lambda_E=0$. Write $H=\sum_Q c_QQ$, let $\ell=\max_{c_Q\ne0}\mathrm{wt}(Q)$, and set $J=\sum_{Q\ne I}|c_Q|$. The logical half-splitting is $\omega=(\lambda_{\max}(h_L)-\lambda_{\min}(h_L))/2$. Some gate pages denote interaction order by $r$; this comparison uses $\ell$ to reserve $r_S$ for syndrome reversibility.

| Result in this account | Scope and additional statement |
|---|---|
| [Interaction-order obstruction](../science/locality.md) | Every such Hamiltonian obeys $\ell\ge d+t$. Correcting all errors through weight $t$ requires $d\ge2t+1$, giving $\ell\ge3t+1$. The argument uses detection, not stabilizer structure or nondegeneracy. |
| [Native coupling constraints](../science/coupling_cost.md) | Weight moments give $J/\omega\ge\sum_{k=0}^t2^k\binom{d+k-1}{k}$ at $\ell=d+t$. For $t=1$ and $\ell>d$, the scalar-moment optimum is $(\ell+d)/(\ell-d)$. These general necessary bounds are not general attainability statements. |
| [Four-body equality example](../science/four_body_construction.md) | Nine commuting terms on a degenerate CSS $[[9,1,3]]$ code attain $\ell=4$ and $J=7\omega$ for one logical axis. This does not give a universal four-body gate set or the smallest possible block. |
| [Growing-distance gates](#concatenated-codes-strict-lifting-and-sparse-descriptions) | On the inherited concatenated five-qubit codes, exact constructions and code-specific moment bounds determine the one-, two-, and three-error optima below, including their native coefficient cost. |
| [Pure logical strings and encoded control](#protected-logical-strings-and-encoded-control) | On unchanged product codes, a specified logical Pauli string has an exact three-error interaction-order and coupling law. The complete protected Hamiltonians provide universal encoded control in the declared model. |

Interaction order counts one native Pauli term. The union of all term supports may contain many more qubits. The coefficient budget depends on the declared physical Pauli basis; it is not an operator norm, a gate count, geometric range, or the cost of synthesizing many-body interactions from two-body controls.

## Inherited foundations and protected-Hamiltonian machinery

Exact correctability and scalar compression of detectable errors are established quantum-error-correction theory: see [Knill and Laflamme (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.55.900) and the stabilizer formalism of [Gottesman (1997)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9705052). Extending a logical Hamiltonian to correctable error sectors, stabilizer dressing, and cancellation between equivalent logical representatives also predate this project. For diagonal Hamiltonians, expansion in $Z$ type Paulis is the usual multilinear Fourier representation; [Hadfield (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3478519) provides a systematic treatment, with degree equal to Pauli interaction order. Interpolation and linear-programming duality are imported tools.

[Vy, Wang, and Jacobs (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/15/5/053002) define error-transparent evolution by matching the logical Hamiltonian in the code and error spaces. Their interaction-order discussion gives a five-body minimum for full single-qubit-error protection. Section II.3 of the arXiv version chooses a common active set of physical qubits and a passive complement, and uses arbitrary logical operations on the relevant sectors. Those conditions differ from one nontrivial continuous axis generated by a sum of terms with different supports. The nine-qubit example here establishes maximum Pauli-term weight four, not arbitrary logical control confined to four physical qubits. Their analysis of slower perturbatively generated many-body dynamics remains a separate implementation question.

[Young, Sarovar, and Blume-Kohout (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.3.041013), Section III.B and Appendix A, construct protected Hamiltonians by stabilizer dressing and a syndrome Fourier transform. Their canonical choice has the same logical generator in every correctable syndrome sector. A broader family permits syndrome-dependent multiplicative deformation. For the $[[5,1,3]]$ code, their canonical Hamiltonian has weight five, while a noncanonical Hamiltonian has weight three. The latter has a syndrome factor proportional to $2P_0+I/2$, where $P_0$ selects the code sector, so an error at an unknown time changes the accumulated logical angle. Such rate changes are different from the additive scalar offsets allowed above. Their construction and high-weight cancellation are antecedents; the additional questions here concern minimum interaction order at fixed logical rate and the native coefficient sum. Their term-count and factorability concerns remain relevant.

[Lebreuilly et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05007), Equation (8), include the strict relation $HEP=EH_0P$ in autonomous correction and computation with engineered dissipation. Their generalized error-transparent Hamiltonians can also move between syndrome spaces while preserving corrected logical dynamics. The bound here assumes static, code-preserving sector intertwining. It is not a lower bound for every Hamiltonian-plus-dissipator scheme: correction strengths, rate separation, and finite-rate error estimates require a separate resource model.

## Other error models and Hamiltonian protection

[Kapit (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.120.050503) develops error-transparent gates for small logical architectures, including repetition-code and very small logical qubit settings. Their error sets and protected sectors follow the device model. Low order for those sets does not imply transparency to all single-qubit Paulis on a distance-three subspace code. [Ma et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-020-0893-x) experimentally realize error-transparent logical phase operations in a bosonic encoding, an operational precedent for matching code and photon-loss evolution. Oscillator number-selective controls have a different resource description from physical-qubit Pauli terms.

[Wetherbee et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2025-10-21-1890), Appendix C, Theorem 3, already give an error-transparent resource lower bound. For continuous amplitude-mixing operations on rotation-symmetric oscillator codes correcting a specified loss and no-jump error set, they require at least $\lfloor l/2\rfloor+1$ squeezing orders, expressed as nonzero odd off-diagonals in parity blocks, under a generalized transparency condition. The present bounds concern a different error family and resource: all tensor-Pauli errors through weight $t$, detection distance $d$, maximum Pauli weight, and the native coefficient sum. They are not claimed to be the first error-transparent resource bounds in general.

Energy penalties and passive protection impose different guarantees:

| Source | Protected object and mechanism | Relation to this model |
|---|---|---|
| [Iman Marvian and Lidar (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.113.260504) | No-go result for protection of an entire degenerate ground space of a commuting two-local Hamiltonian against local perturbations. | The code here need not be the entire ground space of its logical generator. |
| [Milad Marvian and Lidar (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.118.030504) | Subsystem encodings with finite-penalty error bounds and suppression in the large-penalty limit. | Suppressing transitions does not by itself give exact equal-rate evolution after a correctable error. |
| [Milad Marvian and Lloyd (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01354) | Two-body Hamiltonian computation with subsystem gauge penalties, including a bare-logical restriction for a CSS gauge-code class and dressed-operator constructions. | Its restriction depends on the gauge-code and computational-operator setting, without the sector-intertwining hypothesis above. |
| [Singkanipa, Xia, and Lidar (2025)](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2025-08-05-1821) | Distance-two subsystem families for two-body Hamiltonian computation protected by energy penalties. | Coupling geometry and penalty gaps differ from exact correction of all single-qubit errors. |
| [Cao et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07764) | Commuting two-local penalty Hamiltonians with an excited encoding subspace and a distance-two error-detecting code. | The excited encoding avoids the 2014 ground-space hypothesis; protection still depends on a penalty scale and approximation bound. |

A selected final-time gate also differs from a continuous generator: it may exploit phase identities modulo $2\pi$, and ordinary fault tolerance may allow a fault to change to another correctable error. [Nelson et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17612) characterize commuting Pauli-rotation logical operations through syndrome structure. Their angle-dependent codespace conditions and equal-syndrome generator-weight bound do not impose exact sector-by-sector logical-rate matching. [Bradshaw (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05189) studies error-transparent switching along families of permutation-invariant codes. A changing-code protocol, with physical control synthesis still to be supplied, is outside the fixed-code theorem here.

## Concatenated codes, strict lifting, and sparse descriptions

The family $Q_m=[[5^m,1,3^m]]$ is the established concatenated five-qubit code. Canonical protected Hamiltonians supply its base; neither the code, concatenation, nor general stabilizer dressing is attributed to this project. Write $d=3^m$. The derived resource statements are:

| Protected errors | Exact interaction order and budget for one logical Pauli axis | Construction and lower bound |
|---|---|---|
| Weight at most one | $\ell=d+2$, $J/\omega=d+1$ | [Signed recursive classes](../science/minimum_readout_safe_gates.md), with code parity and weight moments. |
| Weight at most two, $m\ge2$ | $\ell=d+4$, $J/\omega=(4d^2+21d+8)/8$ | [Correlated inactive-frontier padding](../science/growing_two_error_gates.md) and bottom-block moments. The [twenty-five-qubit case](../science/two_error_gates.md) has four classes, $\ell=13$, and $J/\omega=521/8$. |
| Weight at most three, $m\ge2$ | $\ell=d+6$, $J/\omega=J_3(d)=(4d^3+51d^2+170d+24)/24$ | [Strict lifting](../science/growing_three_error_gates.md) and a cubic block-moment dual. The [twenty-five-qubit case](../science/three_error_gates.md) has seven classes, $\ell=15$, and $J/\omega=2867/8$. |

Each row gives minimum interaction order and minimum budget at that order for this family and model. Block-resolved moments retain distinctions lost by scalar total-weight moments. In the two-error construction, active/padding correlations are required; a uniform inactive-block average does not preserve them. For three errors, the total-degree truncated tensor combination of nested strict increments gives the full error-image identity: each omitted term has a zero factor on every protected error. The seven-class seed, order-fifteen pattern classification, forced outer weight-five branch, and cubic dual provide the finite resource certificate. In the canonical logical Pauli frame, the three-error cost for a single-block direction is $J_3(d)\sum_\alpha|\Omega_\alpha|$; this is a statement in that prescribed basis.

Sparse description size is a separate result. Standard nonnegative support reduction, as in [Hank Yang's linear-programming background notes](https://hankyang.seas.harvard.edu/Semidefinite/background.html), gives the [single-error existence bound](../science/sparse_protected_gates.md) of $3n+1$ native terms. The code-specific cyclic-orbit and count-vector recursion constructs at most $4n$ terms with $O(\log n)$-bit rational coefficients, including a 75-term twenty-five-qubit witness. The [two-error sparse synthesis](../science/sparse_two_error_synthesis.md) uses positive component certificates and pairwise-independent coupling. Rational torus polygon areas realize unequal pairwise marginals without a denominator-sized grid; the recursive orbit compiler supplies an exact 125-qubit witness. Numerical optimization selects finite support, while rational and integer checks certify coefficients, signed action, and all protected-error responses. For three errors, strict all-depth equality and sparse polynomial-size existence do not yet supply a practical sparse compiler. These are mathematical descriptions and certificates, not hardware feasibility claims.

## Protected logical strings and encoded control

On the fixed product code $Q_m^{\otimes M}$ with $m\ge2$, let $P$ be a specified nonidentity logical Pauli string of logical weight $p$. Its target coset has minimum physical weight $D=pd$, while the ordinary code distance remains $d$. The [logical-string construction](../science/protected_entangling_gates.md) proves, for $h_L=\Omega P+cI$ and $\Omega\ne0$,

```math
\ell_{\min}=D+6,\qquad
J_{\min}(\ell=D+6)=|\Omega|J_3(D).
```

Total-degree truncation of the strict increment hierarchy supplies the Hamiltonian. Projection of the moment equations onto the target logical-Pauli coefficient proves the lower bound against arbitrary competing Hamiltonians, including other logical cosets, detectable terms, stabilizers, and scalar terms. Tensor products and the Pauli Lie-algebra criterion for control are inherited ingredients; the pure-string resource law, protected entangler, and complete finite certificate are the derived statements here. The first $[[50,2,9]]$ logical $ZZ$ instance has $\ell=24$ and $J/\omega=1789$.

Local protected Pauli controls and protected entanglers give universal encoded control through complete Hamiltonians in the declared native model. This statement does not claim that universal protected gates are new in general, that individual sequential native terms remain protected, or that arbitrary mixed logical Hamiltonians attain a common weighted-sum optimum at one shared locality cap. Physical interaction synthesis is a separate task.

## Coexistence with reversible syndrome readout

The companion [Syndrome Reversibility Distance account](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Syndrome-Reversibility-Distance) classifies information loss when a known product of physical single-qubit unitaries acts, the original full stabilizer syndrome is measured ideally, and arbitrary recovery follows with knowledge of the unitary and outcome. Its support criterion and even-logical-circuit characterization give an invariant $r_S$. All the $Q_m$ gate families above and their unchanged product codes retain $r_S=\infty$; for product codes the support-parity argument is blockwise, not a claim that every nontrivial logical string has odd total weight.

The [joint theorem](../science/gate_readout_tradeoff.md) shows that at odd distance, a nontrivial gate attaining the general minimum $\ell=d+t$ forces $r_S=d+1$. Hence $r_S>d+1$, including infinity, requires $\ell\ge d+t+1$. The proof includes reduction of fixed physical qubits. The nine-qubit four-body example has $r_S=4$; the concatenated constructions show coexistence at higher order. The gate guarantee concerns bounded-weight Pauli faults during continuous evolution, whereas the readout guarantee concerns a known coherent product unitary before a prescribed ideal measurement. Neither their coexistence nor encoded universality is a combined noisy threshold.
