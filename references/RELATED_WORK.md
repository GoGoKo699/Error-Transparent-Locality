# Relation to prior work

This project studies exact, continuous logical evolution generated by a static Hamiltonian on a fixed quantum code. The central resource is the largest weight of a term in the Hamiltonian's standard tensor-Pauli expansion. A second resource is the sum of the absolute coefficients in that expansion. These definitions are needed to compare the result with earlier work on error-transparent evolution, protected Hamiltonians, autonomous correction, and energy penalties.

The source versions below were checked on 23 October 2026. Bibliographic entries are provided in [references.bib](references.bib).

## The statement being compared

Let $V$ encode a logical space into physical qubits, and let the code detect every Pauli of weight less than $d$. For each tensor-Pauli error $E$ of weight at most $t$, the Hamiltonian satisfies

```math
HV=Vh_L,\qquad HEV=EVh_L+\lambda_E EV .
```

The logical generator $h_L$ is non-scalar. The real offset $\lambda_E$ may change a branch's overall phase but does not change its logical energy differences. The strict form has every $\lambda_E=0$; the explicit nine-qubit construction satisfies this strict form.

Writing $H=\sum_Q c_Q Q$, the interaction order is $r=\max_{c_Q\ne0}\mathrm{wt}(Q)$, and the coefficient budget is $J(H)=\sum_{Q\ne I}|c_Q|$. The general result is $r\ge d+t$. For $d=3,t=1,r=4$, the coefficient bound is $J(H)\ge7\omega$, where $\omega$ is half the spectral width of $h_L$. A commuting Hamiltonian on a degenerate CSS $[[9,1,3]]$ code attains both bounds for one continuous logical axis.

Interaction order is distinct from the number of physical qubits touched by the whole Hamiltonian. A sum of four-body terms can have different supports whose union contains nine qubits. The coefficient budget also has a declared physical Pauli basis; it is not an operator norm, a gate count, a geometric range, or a cost for synthesizing many-body interactions from two-body controls.

## Frameworks used from earlier work

Exact correctability and the scalar-compression characterization of detectable errors are established quantum-error-correction theory. The relevant foundations are [Knill and Laflamme (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.55.900) and the stabilizer formalism developed in [Gottesman (1997)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9705052). The locality theorem uses the detection property itself; it does not require a stabilizer code or nondegeneracy.

The extension of a desired logical Hamiltonian to its correctable error sectors is established error-transparent-Hamiltonian machinery. Stabilizer dressing and cancellation between equivalent logical representatives also predate this project. For diagonal Hamiltonians, representing an energy function on bit strings by a sum of $Z$ type Pauli operators is the usual multilinear Fourier representation; [Hadfield (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3478519) provides a systematic treatment. The degree of that function is its Pauli interaction order.

The contribution considered here is therefore the code-independent interaction-order constraint, its quantitative coefficient consequences, and exact saturation by the supplied construction. The general sector-extension idea, Fourier representation, interpolation, and linear-programming duality are imported tools.

## Exact error-transparent evolution and the five-body claim

[Vy, Wang, and Jacobs (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/15/5/053002) define error-transparent evolution by matching the logical Hamiltonian in the code and error spaces, and construct it by extending the desired operation over those spaces. Their interaction-order discussion gives a five-body minimum for full single-qubit-error protection.

The proof in Section II.3 of the arXiv version chooses a common active set of $n$ physical qubits and a passive complement on which all the relevant Hamiltonians act trivially. It also uses the ability to perform arbitrary logical operations on the relevant sectors. These are stronger conditions than obtaining one nontrivial continuous logical axis from a sum of terms with differing supports.

The four-local construction here occupies nine physical qubits and establishes one axis. Its precise comparison is that maximum Pauli-term weight four suffices under the stated all-single-Pauli transparency condition. It does not establish arbitrary logical control confined to a common set of four physical qubits. The same earlier paper discusses the slower effective dynamics incurred when many-body interactions are generated perturbatively; the present coefficient bound does not replace that implementation analysis.

## Canonical and deformed protected Hamiltonians

[Young, Sarovar, and Blume-Kohout (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.3.041013), Section III.B and Appendix A, are particularly close prior art. They construct protected Hamiltonians through stabilizer dressing and an error-syndrome Fourier transform. Their canonical choice implements the same logical generator in every correctable syndrome sector. A broader family permits syndrome-dependent multiplicative deformation of the logical generator.

For the $[[5,1,3]]$ code, the canonical construction has weight five, while a noncanonical construction reduces the weight to three. The latter has a syndrome factor proportional to $2P_0+I/2$, where $P_0$ selects the code sector. Consequently the logical rate changes between the code and error sectors. An error at an unknown time changes the accumulated logical angle. This is different from the additive scalar offsets allowed in the theorem above.

This distinction reconciles their weight-three example with $r\ge d+t$. Their construction and high-weight cancellation method are established antecedents; the additional issue here is the minimum interaction order when the logical rate is fixed across error sectors. Their discussion also identifies term counts and factorability as implementation concerns, without giving the native-Pauli coefficient bound used here.

## Strict transparency and autonomous computation

[Lebreuilly et al. (2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05007) characterize autonomous error correction and protected computation using engineered dissipation. Their Equation (8) includes a construction obeying the strict relation $HEP=EH_0P$. They also develop a broader class of generalized error-transparent Hamiltonians, including Hamiltonians that move between syndrome spaces while preserving the desired corrected logical dynamics.

The theorem in this repository applies to the explicitly stated static, code-preserving, sector-intertwining condition. A generalized autonomous computation need not satisfy that condition. Its protection is assessed in conjunction with engineered recovery and a separation of rates. The allowed logical-drive scale relative to the correction strength, and the resulting finite-rate error estimates, are separate quantities from $J(H)$.

It follows that $r\ge d+t$ is not a lower bound for every Hamiltonian-plus-dissipator scheme that implements a protected logical evolution. Extending the resource theorem to such schemes would require a separate definition and proof.

## Resource bounds and implementations in other error models

[Kapit (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.120.050503) develops error-transparent gates for small logical architectures, including a repetition-code setting and the very small logical qubit architecture. Those constructions are tied to the errors and protected sectors of the particular device model. A low interaction order in such a restricted model does not establish transparency to all single-qubit Paulis on a distance-three subspace code.

[Ma et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-020-0893-x) experimentally implement error-transparent logical phase operations in a bosonic encoding. This provides an operational precedent for matching the desired evolution in code and photon-loss error spaces. Oscillator number-selective controls have a different resource description from a physical-qubit Pauli expansion.

[Wetherbee et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2025-10-21-1890) give a particularly relevant resource lower bound. Appendix C, Theorem 3, treats continuous amplitude-mixing operations on rotation-symmetric oscillator codes correcting a specified loss and no-jump error set. It requires at least $\lfloor l/2\rfloor+1$ squeezing orders, expressed as nonzero odd off-diagonals in the parity blocks of the Hamiltonian. The theorem allows a generalized transparency condition.

Thus resource lower bounds for error-transparent gates already exist. The present claim concerns a different error family and resource: all tensor-Pauli errors through weight $t$, detection distance $d$, maximum tensor-Pauli weight, and the native Pauli coefficient sum. A claim to the first error-transparent resource bound in general would be inappropriate.

## Hamiltonian error suppression and locality restrictions

Several results constrain Hamiltonian protection under different physical guarantees. Their distinctions matter when comparing small interaction orders.

| Source | Protected object and mechanism | Relation to the present statement |
|---|---|---|
| [Iman Marvian and Lidar (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.113.260504) | An entire degenerate ground space of a commuting two-local Hamiltonian; a no-go result for protection against local perturbations. | The hypothesis concerns the ground space and passive energy protection. The code here need not be the entire ground space of its logical generator. |
| [Milad Marvian and Lidar (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.118.030504) | Subsystem encodings with an energy penalty; finite-penalty error bounds and suppression in the large-penalty limit. | The penalty strength suppresses transitions. Exact equal-rate logical evolution after a correctable error is a separate requirement. |
| [Milad Marvian and Lloyd (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01354) | Two-body Hamiltonian computation with subsystem gauge penalties; a restriction on bare logical operators for a class of CSS gauge codes, and constructions using dressed operators. | Its locality restriction depends on the gauge-code and computational-operator setting. It does not impose the sector-intertwining condition above. |
| [Singkanipa, Xia, and Lidar (2025)](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2025-08-05-1821) | Distance-two subsystem families for two-body Hamiltonian computation protected by energy penalties. | Rate, coupling geometry, and penalty gaps are central resources. Distance-two detection with energy suppression does not give the all-single-error exact correction guarantee studied here. |
| [Cao et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07764) | Commuting two-local penalty Hamiltonians with an excited encoding subspace and a distance-two error-detecting code. | Moving away from ground-space encoding avoids the assumption of the 2014 no-go result. The protection remains controlled by a penalty scale and an approximation bound. |

These works demonstrate why neither the word “local” nor the phrase “error protected” determines a comparison by itself. Maximum interaction order, geometric range, the role of ancillas or gauge degrees of freedom, and the error guarantee must all be specified.

## Continuous generators, final-time gates, and moving codes

A final-time code-preserving gate can rely on phase identities modulo $2\pi$. A fixed-code continuous generator must satisfy the relevant operator identities throughout its evolution. In addition, ordinary fault tolerance can allow a fault to change to another correctable error, whereas strict transparency retains its intertwining with the logical evolution.

[Nelson et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17612) characterize logical operations assembled from commuting Pauli rotations through their syndrome structure. Their angle-dependent codespace-preservation conditions, and a generator-weight bound for an equal-syndrome gate setting, concern a different criterion from exact sector-by-sector logical-rate matching. A code-preserving gate at a selected angle does not by itself provide a generator satisfying the theorem's hypotheses.

[Bradshaw (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05189) studies error-transparent switching between permutation-invariant codes along continuous families. That work concerns a changing code and leaves physical control synthesis as an additional problem. The fixed-code locality theorem here does not provide a lower bound for an arbitrary moving-code protocol.

## Growing-distance gates with reversible syndrome readout

The [concatenated equality construction](../science/minimum_readout_safe_gates.md) uses the inherited five-qubit code and canonical protected Hamiltonian as its base. It combines recursively chosen signed Pauli classes with the existing weight-moment and syndrome-reversibility theorems. Its derived statement is that $[[5^m,1,3^m]]$ codes admit strictly single-error-transparent gates on each logical Pauli axis with optimal interaction order $d+2$ and native budget $(d+1)\omega$, while retaining $r_S=\infty$. The code family and the general idea of stabilizer dressing are not attributed to this construction. The statement is established here by its explicit proof and exact certificates; this does not constitute an exhaustive literature-priority claim.

## Sparse descriptions and exact support reduction

The [sparse gate account](../science/sparse_protected_gates.md) uses the standard fact that a feasible nonnegative linear system admits independent support no larger than its rank; see, for example, the [linear-programming background notes by Hank Yang](https://hankyang.seas.harvard.edu/Semidefinite/background.html). The proof is included directly. This principle supplies the $3n+1$ native-term bound. The code-specific construction then uses coordinatewise cyclic automorphisms, low-dimensional count vectors, and exact coupling of finite distributions to generate at most $4n$ terms with $O(\log n)$-bit rational coefficients. Those are derived statements about this protected-gate family, with no claim that the general support-reduction principle is new. A closed 75-term twenty-five-qubit witness and the arbitrary-depth algorithm have separate exact certificates.
